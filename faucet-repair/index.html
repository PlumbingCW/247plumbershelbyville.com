A valve that will not shut off

The scariest version of this call is a water supply that cannot be stopped. A stop valve under a sink or toilet has turned slowly for years, then snaps or spins freely when you try to close it. Water keeps running into the cabinet, and the next shutoff upstream is the main.

Go to the main valve first. Turn it clockwise until it stops. In most Shelbyville houses the main sits where the pipe enters near the water heater, in the basement or crawlspace. If your house main will not move either, the old valve is the real problem, and we can replace it along with the fixture valve.

Handle spins, nothing closes

The stem has stripped or the washer inside has failed. The valve needs to be replaced.

Valve weeps at the handle

A slow drip at the stem often means a dried packing nut that can be snugged, or a worn seal.

Crusted blue-green corrosion

Mineral buildup around a compression nut means a slow leak and a valve near the end of its life.

Water sprays under the sink

A cracked supply line or a split valve body. Close the main and call.

Valve stuck wide open

Mineral scale has locked the stem. Forcing it can snap the stem or twist the pipe.

Dripping, spraying and weak faucets

Most faucet complaints are inside parts, not the whole fixture. A single-handle faucet drips because the cartridge wore out. An older two-handle faucet drips because a rubber washer or a seat is scored. A pull-down kitchen spout sprays sideways when the aerator or the hose fitting clogs.

Weak flow from one tap is usually the aerator. Unscrew it, rinse out the grit, and soak it in vinegar if it is crusted. Test your tap if scale keeps coming back, and ask Indiana American Water for its annual water quality report.

Repair the faucet

  • Cartridge, washer or seat swapped in a short visit
  • Right for a fixture you like and that is otherwise sound
  • Parts must still be available for the model
  • Lower upfront cost

Replace the faucet

  • Better when the body is pitted, cracked or corroded
  • Fixes several complaints at once
  • Lets you choose a new style and flow rate
  • Often paired with new stop valves

When you replace a faucet, check the box for marks. ASME A112.18.1 is the standard for plumbing supply fittings, and NSF/ANSI 61 with NSF/ANSI 372 covers drinking water contact and low lead content, which matters most on a kitchen tap. EPA WaterSense bathroom faucets flow at 1.5 gallons per minute or less, against the federal maximum of 2.2.

Tubs, showers and laundry valves

Shower valves fail from the inside. A cartridge that no longer seals will leave the showerhead dribbling long after you turn it off, or swing between scalding and cold. Replacing it can mean a small access panel in the wall behind the tub. Ask for a pressure-balancing or thermostatic valve that meets ASSE 1016, which is built to limit scalding swings.

A tub spout that leaks from the diverter, a laundry sink with a loose supply, and the two valves behind a washing machine all follow the same rule. If a valve has not been turned in years, replace it while the water is off, not after it fails.

Why valves fail in older Shelbyville houses

Houses in the West Side Historic District, built between about 1853 and 1939, often still have old brass or chrome stop valves and galvanized supply stubs that have been painted over many times. Corrosion inside the pipe can make the whole stub brittle. If you own a house that age, ask the plumber to look at the pipe the valve screws onto, not just the valve.

In any house, a valve that sits unused for years seizes. Turning each stop valve a quarter turn twice a year keeps the washer from sticking.

Turn the water back on slowly, run hot and cold, and watch every joint for a bead of water.

Before we arrive at a valve or faucet break

Four moves that keep the water out of the floor.

1

Close the house main

Clockwise to stop. If it is a lever, turn it so the handle sits across the pipe.

2

Open a low faucet

A tub or basement spigot drains pressure out of the pipes and slows leaks from the broken part.

3

Catch and mop

Put a bucket or towels under the break. Standing water near a refrigerator, outlet or power strip needs the breaker off.

4

Keep the broken part

Save the old valve or cartridge. Matching the size and thread is faster with it in hand.

What changes the price of a faucet or valve job

A small valve can be a fast fix or a bigger job depending on what is behind it.

Part and brandA standard stop valve costs little. A designer shower trim or a discontinued cartridge costs more and can take time to find.
Pipe condition behind itA clean copper stub takes a new valve easily. Brittle galvanized may need a section of pipe replaced.
AccessUnder-sink cabinets are quick. A shower valve behind tile needs a panel or tile removal.
Number of fixturesReplacing every old stop valve in the house at one visit costs less per valve than separate calls.
Time of the callAfter-hours dispatch for a flooding supply line is priced differently from a planned daytime repair.

Homeowner policies usually cover the water damage from a sudden supply line failure but not the worn faucet or valve itself. Check your policy language and call your agent before you file.
