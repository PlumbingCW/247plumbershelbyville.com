Waldron on the county map: east side, rural roads, its own water district

Waldron is not a town. It is a census-designated place in Liberty Township, so there is no town hall behind it. As an unincorporated area, it falls under the Shelby County Building Inspector for building and plumbing permits. Permit rules vary, so ask the building department before work. The Indiana Plumbing Commission licenses plumbing contractors, and the state's lookup at mylicense.in.gov shows whether a license is active.

Water here does not come from Shelbyville's utility. Waldron has its own public water supplier, the Waldron Conservancy District, which draws groundwater from its own wells. Shelbyville's customers are served by Indiana American Water instead. If water goes out, call the right utility first, then us for what is inside your house. Repairs here are made to the same Indiana Plumbing Code standard as in Shelbyville, though which office inspects the work depends on the address.

Spring thaw, soaked ground and the septic question

We found no evidence of a public sewer for Waldron, so if your home is on a septic system, wet ground changes how it behaves. A saturated field cannot take more water, and slow drains in every fixture at once, gurgling or wet spots over the tank are the signs. The Shelby County Health Department, Environmental Division, issues septic permits (the fee is $300), inspects repairs, and keeps records back to 1955.

Do not keep running the washer and hoping. Cutting water use gives the field time to dry. Our sewer line cleaning visit clears the house side, and we tell you when the problem is the tank or field, which needs a county inspector.

What we could not confirm about Waldron sewer

Water from the Conservancy District, and what to ask it

Because the Waldron Conservancy District supplies its own groundwater, its water quality report is the district's, not Shelbyville's. We could not find a published hardness figure. Test your tap with a hardness strip, and ask the district for its annual water quality report before you spend money on a softener.

Low pressure on a district connection can be a closed curb valve, a failing pressure reducer in your house or a leak in your service line. Check the pressure with a gauge on an outdoor bib. If the gauge reads low, call us, and see our water service line repair page for how a buried leak is found.

Before we arrive in Waldron

Four steps for district-water and rural homes.

1

Close the house main

It is usually where the line enters near the meter or pressure tank. If the main will not close, the curb valve is usually the district's, so call them.

2

Cut water to the septic system

If the drains are slow in every fixture, stop the washer and dishwasher until the system is checked.

3

Keep clear of wet electrical areas

Pump controls, the water heater and the panel are dangerous in standing water.

4

Tell dispatch your water source
