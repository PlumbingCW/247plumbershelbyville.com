Sewage coming up in the house

A sewer backup is an emergency because the water is not clean and it keeps coming. If sewage rises in a floor drain, a tub or the lowest toilet, stop using every fixture and call the 24/7 line. The sooner the plumber sees the line, the smaller the cleanup.

We send a licensed plumber with a cable and a camera. Same-day appointments are offered when available, and night and weekend calls are handled by the same line. We also serve Fairland and nearby towns.

Water or sewage in the basement

The lowest drain is the first place a blocked main shows itself. Sewage here means the line is full to the house.

Gurgling toilets and tubs

When a toilet bubbles after the washer drains, air and water are fighting for the same shared pipe.

Slow drains at every fixture

Upstairs and downstairs slow together means the problem is below all of them.

Sewer smell in the yard

A damp, sunken or greener patch over the line points to a leak in the pipe.

Repeat backups after cabling

A cable that clears the line and fails within weeks says the pipe is cracked, bellied or full of roots.

Do these while the plumber is on the way

Sewage backups carry bacteria, so keep it simple and keep clear.

1

Stop all water use

No flushing, showers, dishwasher or washer. Each gallon you send down comes back up at the lowest drain.

2

Keep out of the water

Wear rubber boots and gloves if you must cross it. Keep children and pets away and do not use a household vacuum on sewage.

3

Open the area to air

Open a window if the smell is strong. Leave the house if anyone feels sick or dizzy.

4

Photograph for your insurer

Take pictures of the water line and the damage before anything is moved. Your policy may cover backup damage only with a sewer backup endorsement.

How we find where the line is broken

1

Cable the blockage

If sewage is backing up, the first move is to open the line with cleaning so the house is usable. The cable head also brings back clues: soil, roots, scale or pipe chips.

2

Run the sewer camera

A waterproof camera on a push rod shows cracks, offset joints, a belly that holds water, root intrusion or a collapse. Footage is saved so you can see it. Many utilities describe defects in NASSCO's PACP coding, and asking for the same terms makes quotes easier to compare.

3

Locate the break

A transmitter on the camera head lets the plumber trace the spot from the surface and measure its depth and distance from the cleanout.

4

Choose the repair method

The plumber explains the options with the footage in front of you. Digging, lining and pipe bursting each fit different damage.

Is trenchless lining right for your pipe?

Lining pulls a resin-soaked felt tube into the old pipe, inflates it, and cures it to form a new pipe inside the old one. It shrinks the inside diameter a little, so the camera must show the host pipe is round and clear of heavy offsets first. ASTM F1216 is the standard practice for this resin-tube method, so ask which standard and wall thickness the proposed liner is designed to.

Lining works well

  • Hairline cracks and open joints
  • Root entry at sound pipe
  • Long runs under driveways and landscaping
  • Pipe still holds its shape

Dig or burst instead

  • Collapsed or crushed sections
  • A belly that holds water
  • Severe offsets at joints
  • Branch connections that cannot be reinstated

Cast iron sewer pipe in older homes

Cast iron served well for decades, but it rusts from the inside. Scale narrows the bore, and the pipe eventually cracks along the bottom where waste sits. Pre-1940 houses, like many of the 373 contributing buildings in the West Side Historic District, can still have cast iron and clay drains under floors and yards.

That is why a sewer camera inspection is cheaper than guessing. Short sections of failed cast iron can be cut out and replaced with PVC. Whole-line failure usually points to lining or replacement, and epoxy patching is a short-term fix at best.

If you also have rusted supply lines, repiping can be planned alongside the work. If the cast iron runs under a slab or through a finished basement, ask about lining first, since it avoids breaking concrete. If a long run is rotting, a full replacement also lets you move the cleanout to a better spot.

Repairs still need a permit in the city. Only registered plumbing contractors can apply for plumbing permits in Shelbyville, and the work is held to the Indiana Plumbing Code, which the city lists as the 2012 edition. Ask the plumber to confirm replacement PVC drain pipe is marked to an ASTM standard such as D2665, and to show you the permit before the trench is backfilled.

Septic systems in Shelby County

Not every Shelbyville-area home is on city sewer. In Shelby County, the Health Department's Environmental Division issues permits for residential and commercial sewage disposal systems that are not connected to a regulated sewage facility. The permit fee is $300, and all repairs and new construction must be inspected and signed off by county inspectors.

The county keeps septic records back to 1955, so ask the Health Department before you dig. Septic tanks and drain fields are a different job from a sewer lateral, so tell dispatch which system you have when you call.
