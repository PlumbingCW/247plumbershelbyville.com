No water, weak water, or a pump that never shuts off

If your Fairland home is on a private well, a dry tap usually points to the well pump, the pressure switch or the pressure tank, not the street. A tank pressure gauge that swings quickly between low and high, with the pump clicking on every few seconds, means a waterlogged tank. Check your breaker first. Do not open the pressure switch cover with wet hands or while standing in water.

If the water is running but weak, a failing pump, a clogged sediment filter or a leak in the buried line from well to house can all do it. A buried leak is tracked with acoustic listening and a pressure test. See our water service line repair page for how that digging is scoped before anyone runs a trencher.

Repairs here are made to the same Indiana Plumbing Code standard as in Shelbyville, though which office inspects the work depends on the address.

Slow drains and gurgling when you are not sure where the waste goes

Many rural homes use a septic system, not a sewer main. In Shelby County, the county health department's Environmental Division issues permits for sewage disposal systems that are not connected to a regulated sewer, and repairs have to be inspected and signed off. The septic permit fee is $300. The department keeps records back to 1955, so ask for your property's file before anyone digs.

A septic backup looks like slow drains in every fixture, gurgling in the toilet when the washer drains, and wet or foul-smelling ground over the tank or field. Never run a jetter blindly into a septic line. We locate the tank, check the baffle and the line to the field, and say whether the fix is a pump-out, a cleared line or a permitted repair. Our sewer line repair page covers the in-house side of that line.

A wet basement, and a sump pump that quits when you need it

Brandywine Creek runs through Brandywine Township. We have no data on flooding in Fairland itself, so this is practical, not predictive: any basement below grade, in any county, depends on its sump pit during a hard rain. The pump, the float switch and the discharge line each fail in different ways, and a stuck float is the cheapest repair.

Lift the pit lid and watch the float rise. If water is rising and the pump is silent, check the outlet and the breaker. A battery backup keeps pumping if the power goes out in the same storm. For a failed pump or a first install, see sump pump repair and installation.

Before we arrive in Fairland

Four steps that fit a well, septic or town-water house.

1

Shut the water at the right place

On a well, switch off the pump breaker and close the valve after the pressure tank. On public water, close the house main near the meter.

2

Stop sending water down the drain

On septic, extra water pushes more waste toward the field. No laundry, no long showers, until the system is checked.

3

Move people and valuables away from standing water

Keep clear of the sump pit, the water heater and electrical outlets if the floor is wet.

4

Gather what dispatch will ask

Well or public water, septic or sewer, where the shutoff is and what you hear or see. It speeds up the first visit.
