Why the oldest blocks in Morristown need a different first look

Morristown was platted in 1828, and its Junction Railroad Depot is on the National Register of Historic Places. A town that old has houses from many decades, and each decade left its own pipe. Galvanized steel supply lines, cast iron or clay drains and later copper or plastic patches can all sit in one basement.

We cannot name your pipe material from the road. A licensed plumber can usually tell in minutes with a magnet (it sticks to galvanized steel, not copper), a scratch test and a look at the crawl space. That decides the repair: a pinhole in corroded steel often means the rest of the run is thin, so a section replacement can beat a clamp. Our burst pipe repair work on old steel is a different job from soldering new copper.

Repairs here are made to the same Indiana Plumbing Code standard as in Shelbyville, though which office inspects the work depends on the address.

Rusty water after a quiet night

Run the cold tap for a minute. If it clears fast, it was sitting water. If it stays brown, the inside of an old steel line is shedding rust.

Strong flow at one tap, weak at another

Mineral scale narrows old galvanized pipe from the inside. The fixture at the end of the longest run feels it first.

Snaking helps for a week, then the drain slows again

Scale in cast iron or roots in clay tile catch new debris. A sewer camera shows which one it is.

Gurgling and a sewer smell near the main stack

A partly blocked main or vent pulls air through your traps. Stop running laundry and dishwasher until it is checked.

A damp strip on the basement wall facing the street

That points to the water service line between the meter and the house. Listen at the meter with every tap off for movement.

Town water and town sewer: who owns which pipe

Morristown runs its own water department and its own wastewater department with a treatment plant. Most in-town homes therefore sit on town water and town sewer, not a well and septic tank. The first question on any emergency is which side of the property line the failure is on. Any dig to reach a buried line needs a call to Indiana 811 at least two business days ahead.

Freeze, thaw and the frost line

Indiana winters push frost into the ground. The City of Shelbyville lists a 30 inch frost depth in its building codes, and Morristown shares the same freeze and thaw pattern, so ask the Morristown building authority for the number it uses. The practical point: any line buried shallower than frost depth, any hose bib on an exterior wall and any pipe in an unheated crawl space can split.

A split shows up as a spray when the ice thaws, not while it is frozen. If you find a frozen pipe, close the main valve before it thaws and call the 24/7 line. A failed tank during a cold snap is the other common call, and our water heater repair visits start with the relief valve, the burner or element and the anode condition.

Before we arrive in Morristown

Four things that limit the damage while the plumber is on the way.

1

Close the house main

It is usually where the line enters the basement or crawl space, near the meter. Turn the handle clockwise. If it will not turn, do not force it.

2

Stop using every drain if the sewer is backing up

Town sewer backups travel to the lowest fixture, often a basement floor drain or shower. No laundry, no flushing.

3

Keep people away from wet electrical areas

Do not stand in water near outlets, a panel or a water heater. Switch off breakers only if you can reach them while dry.

4

Check whether neighbours are affected

Whole-street trouble is a town system problem. Tell dispatch either way, because it changes where the plumber looks first.
