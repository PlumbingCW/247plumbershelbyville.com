The first ten minutes of a burst pipe

Water from a split pipe can run at several gallons a minute, so minutes matter more than anything else. Shut the water off at the main valve, open a faucet on the lowest floor to drain the lines, and call. Then deal with the rest.

Our line runs 24/7 and takes calls at night, on weekends and on holidays. Same-day appointments are offered when available, and dispatch can talk you through the shutoff while the plumber is on the way.

Do this before the plumber arrives

Four steps that limit the flooding.

1

Close the main shutoff

It is usually a ball valve or wheel handle where the water line enters the house, often in the basement or utility room. Turn it clockwise until it stops. Leave the street-side meter valve alone.

2

Drain the lines

Open the lowest faucet and flush a toilet once. This empties the pipes and drops the pressure feeding the leak.

3

Cut power to wet areas

If water reaches outlets, appliances or the electrical panel, do not touch them wet. Leave and call a licensed electrician for anything near wiring.

4

Move belongings and photograph

Lift furniture, rugs and boxes. Take photos of the damage for your insurer. Start mopping or pumping only after the water is off and the area is safe.

Water on a ceiling or wall

Stains that spread, a bulge in drywall or dripping from a light fixture point to a supply line inside the wall or floor above.

Hissing or rushing sound

With every tap closed, a steady hiss means water is escaping under pressure somewhere in the house.

No water or low pressure

A burst line or a broken supply valve can drop pressure suddenly. If water is off at one fixture but on at others, one branch failed.

Wet floor or soggy yard

Water seeping through the slab or pooling over the buried line points to a break outside the house.

Meter running with taps closed

The dial turns when no one uses water. The leak is real, even if it is not visible.

Frozen pipes: thaw safely or wait for the plumber

Water expands about 9 percent when it freezes, and a pipe splits where ice blocks it. The crack often shows up only after the thaw, when water starts running again. Pipes on exterior walls, in crawlspaces and in unheated garages freeze first.

If a faucet runs only a trickle in a hard freeze, open it and leave it open. Warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer, never an open flame, and shut the main off if you spot a crack or a wet spot.

Safe to try yourself

  • Open the faucet so melting ice can flow
  • Warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer or warm towels
  • Heat the room, open cabinet doors under sinks
  • Shut the main off at the first sign of a leak

Call the plumber for

  • A visible crack, bulge or spray
  • Frozen pipe in a wall or under a slab
  • No access to the frozen section
  • Any case where you smell gas. Leave, call 911 and your gas utility

How deep frost reaches in Shelbyville

The City of Shelbyville lists a frost line depth of 30 inches in its building code design criteria. Buried water lines that sit below that depth are protected from ground frost, while shallow lines, exposed pipes and meter pits are not.

If your home had a line freeze, ask a plumber to check the burial depth of the service line, because a shallow run will freeze again in the next hard winter.

How the plumber finds and fixes the break

1

Confirm the water is off

The plumber checks your main valve and, if it fails, the meter shutoff, so repairs happen on a dry pipe.

2

Trace the leak

Wall and ceiling leaks are found by moisture meter and thermal imaging. A buried leak is found by pressure gauge and acoustic listening on the line.

3

Cut out the damaged section

The split is cut back to sound pipe, usually a few inches either side, so the repair does not sit on weak material.

4

Replace with the right material

Copper with sweat or press fittings, PEX with crimp rings or push fittings, or CPVC for hot and cold runs. The plumber matches pipe to what is already in the house.

5

Pressure test and restore

The line is refilled, checked for leaks at pressure, and the plumber shows you which walls were opened so you can arrange drywall and drying.

Replacement pipe should carry its standard marking. Copper tube is made to ASTM B88, PEX tubing to ASTM F876 and CPVC hot and cold water pipe to ASTM D2846, and anything touching drinking water should be certified to NSF/ANSI 61. Ask the plumber to show you the markings on the pipe and fittings used before the wall is closed.

Keep it from happening again

  • Insulate pipes in crawlspaces, garages and exterior walls with foam sleeves.
  • Disconnect garden hoses and shut off outdoor spigots before the first hard freeze.
  • Open the cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls on cold nights.
  • Know where your main valve is, and test that it turns before you need it.
  • If you leave for a cold week, keep the heat on, and have someone check the house.
