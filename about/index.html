How the 24/7 dispatch works

We take calls by phone, day and night, and send licensed plumbers to the address you give. The address on this site, 405 N Harrison St in Shelbyville, is our business address and not a shop counter. That is why the first step is always a call.

1

You call the line

Say what is happening, whether water is still running and where you are.

2

Dispatch sorts the call

Active leaks, sewage backups and heat or hot water outages come first. Dispatch gives you the safe first steps while the visit is arranged.

3

A licensed plumber is sent

The plumber finds the cause and explains the options and the price.

4

You approve, then work starts

Nothing is repaired or replaced until you have agreed to the price.

What happens when you call the 24/7 line

Dispatch asks plain questions first: what is happening, where the water is going, and the street address with the town. Those three answers decide how your call is sorted, so say them in that order if you can. Then expect a few follow-up questions that match your problem.

  • Water you can see: dispatch asks whether it is still running and whether you can reach the main shutoff, usually near the water meter or where the line enters the house.
  • Drains and sewage: dispatch asks which fixtures are affected. One slow sink is a branch clog, while a toilet, tub and floor drain backing up together points at the main sewer line.
  • No hot water: dispatch asks whether the water heater is gas or electric and whether there is any water on the floor around the tank.
  • Safety: a gas smell or water near live electrical outlets goes to 911 first, before any plumbing call.

The call ends with a plan for the visit and the safe steps to take until the plumber is there. No arrival time is promised, because storms, traffic and the number of active calls change it.

What to have ready before you call

The full address

Street, town and ZIP. Shelbyville uses 46176, while Morristown, Fairland and Waldron each have their own ZIP, and naming the town avoids mix ups.

What you already tried

A plunger, a closed valve, a reset breaker. Tell dispatch so the plumber does not repeat it or work around it.

Where the trouble is

The kitchen, the basement floor drain, the second floor bath. The location shapes which tools go on the truck.

The water heater label

For a heater problem, read the model and size off the rating plate on the tank if you can reach it safely.

Clear access

A path to the under-sink cabinet, the basement stairs or the heater closet, with pets put away.

Your phone nearby

Keep the phone you called from close, since dispatch may need to ask one more question.

What to check about any plumber

Indiana licenses plumbing contractors through the Indiana Plumbing Commission. The state's license lookup at mylicense.in.gov can be searched by name, license number, status, city, county or ZIP, and it takes a minute. Check us, and check anyone else you are considering.

  • Ask whether the work needs a permit. In the city of Shelbyville, only registered plumbing contractors can apply for plumbing permits.
  • Ask whether the work follows the Indiana Plumbing Code, which the City of Shelbyville lists as the 2012 edition and the building department enforces.
  • Ask for the price before work starts, in plain words, and what could change it.
  • Be wary of a plumber who offers to skip a permit that the work needs.
  • For rules in Morristown, Fairland, Waldron or the county, ask the local building department.

Verifying a plumber in four steps

1

Search the state lookup

Go to mylicense.in.gov and search by name, license number, status, city, county or ZIP. You want an active plumbing contractor license under the Indiana Plumbing Commission.

2

Ask about city registration

The City of Shelbyville requires plumbing contractors to register with its Building Commission before pulling permits. Registration needs proof of an active state license, a liability insurance certificate, a $10,000 surety bond and a $100 fee.

3

Ask who pulls the permit

In the city, only plumbing contractors can apply for and obtain plumbing permits. A homeowner doing work on a home they own and live in must get a permit before beginning any work.

4

Hear the price in words

Ask what is included, what could change the number, and whether a camera, parts or a street cut might add to it. Then decide.

Outside the city, the rules come from the town or the county. Morristown handles its own plumbing permits through town government, and the Shelby County Building Inspector serves the unincorporated county and Fairland, so a short call to the right office answers it.

What we do not do

  • We do not promise a set arrival time. Traffic, weather and the number of active calls decide it.
  • We do not take requests through a website, chat or email. Every job starts with a phone call.
  • We do not work on gas utility lines or electrical wiring. For a gas smell, leave the building and call 911 and your gas utility. For wiring, hire a licensed electrician.
  • We do not start work until you have agreed to the price.

How prices work without a quote form

There is no online estimate tool, because a drain clog, a failing heater element and a cracked sewer pipe cannot be priced from a text box. The price is agreed by phone before work starts, once the plumber has seen the problem and explained the options. You hear the number in words, ask what could change it, and say yes or no before anything is cut, drained or replaced.
