Where the 24/7 line sends plumbers

Home base is Shelbyville, ZIP 46176, which is the only ZIP the city uses. From there the line reaches three towns with their own pages: Morristown to the north-northeast, Fairland to the northwest and Waldron to the east-southeast. Each page covers that town's water, sewer and housing questions.

Smaller places get calls too. Marietta, Flat Rock, Boggstown, Fountaintown, London and Norristown do not have pages of their own, but residents there can call the same line and give a street address. Dispatch confirms the address before sending anyone.

Shelbyville sits at the fork of the Big Blue and Little Blue Rivers, so many calls involve sump pumps and wet basements. Out in the country, wells and septic systems are common, which is why sewer line repair questions often start with whether a home is on a septic system or a public sewer. Septic permits come from the Shelby County Health Department.

The Shelbyville home zone

Every Shelbyville street address uses ZIP 46176, the only ZIP in the city. Walkerville, an unincorporated community inside the city limits, is covered as part of Shelbyville, and Marietta residents call the same line and give a street address. Give dispatch the town name along with the number, since a few county ZIPs sit close by.

The city sits at the fork of the Big Blue and Little Blue Rivers. The city flood page treats yards near the Big Blue as flooding once the river reaches 16.3 feet, and homes near CR 100N and Boggstown Road at 17 feet. It names those roads, Sunset Park and Bear Chase Golf Course as high-risk flood areas, so test your sump pump before the river rises if you live there. Read about basement waterproofing for the long-term fix.

The Shelbyville Commercial Historic District runs roughly along Broadway, Tompkins, Mechanic and Noble Streets, with 149 contributing buildings dating from about 1822 to the 1930s. Buildings that old often have cast iron, galvanized and later copper or PVC drains in one run, so a sewer camera inspection shows what is there before anyone digs.

The West Side Historic District, roughly between W. Pennsylvania, N. Harrison, W. Hendricks and N. Conrey, has 373 contributing buildings developed from about 1853 to 1939. Houses from that era can still carry galvanized supply lines or old cast iron and clay drains, so low pressure and repeat clogs there are worth a look at the pipe itself, and repiping is the permanent answer when the pipe is the problem.

I-74, US 421, SR 9 and SR 44 all serve Shelbyville. When you call, name the road you are on or the nearest cross street, which helps dispatch place a rural address quickly.

Town by town

Morristown lies north-northeast of Shelbyville in Hanover Township, ZIP 46161. It is an incorporated town with its own water and wastewater departments, so a sewer backup raises the question of whether the blockage is in your lateral or in the town's main. Fountaintown (46130) and Norristown are nearby names that residents can use when they call.

Fairland is northwest of Shelbyville in Brandywine Township, ZIP 46126, about 6 to 8 miles out. We do not state which utility serves each address, so ask your water provider, and check whether your home is on a well and septic system. Boggstown (46110) is the nearby mention-only name.

Waldron is east-southeast of Shelbyville in Liberty Township, ZIP 46182. It is an unincorporated community, not a town, and its public water comes from the Waldron Conservancy District, a different utility from Shelbyville's. If your home is on a septic system, the septic rules below apply. Flat Rock (47234) and London are the neighboring names.

Septic is separate. The Shelby County Health Department Environmental Division issues permits for residential and commercial sewage disposal systems not connected to a regulated sewage facility. The permit is $300, repairs and new construction must be inspected and signed off, and records go back to 1955, which helps if you are buying a home and want the history of its system.

In every one of these places the work is held to the Indiana Plumbing Code, a statewide rule, so the office you ask is checking the same code. If the job involves digging, Indiana 811 takes requests around the clock and wants at least two business days of notice.
