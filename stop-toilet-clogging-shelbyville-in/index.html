Stop a Toilet From Clogging: Shelbyville, IN Homeowner Guide

To stop a toilet from clogging in Shelbyville, IN, flush only toilet paper and human waste, use a flange plunger when it does clog, and treat clogs that return within a month as a sign of a weak flush or a partly blocked drain line.

Why one toilet clogs again and again

A toilet is designed to move about 1.6 gallons through a trapway about 2 inches wide, fast enough to carry waste into the drain. When it clogs repeatedly, one of three things is wrong. The load is too much for the flush, the flush itself is weak, or the drain beyond the toilet is partly blocked.

Think of it as three suspects, checked in order. Habits first, hardware second, pipe third. Most clogs end at the first suspect, which is why the habits section comes before any repair talk.

Match the symptom to the suspect

What belongs in the toilet, and what does not

Safe to flush

  • Human waste
  • Toilet paper, in reasonable amounts
  • Nothing else, even if the package says flushable

Never flush

  • Wipes of any kind, including baby and cleaning wipes
  • Paper towels, tissues and cotton balls
  • Dental floss, hair and cat litter
  • Grease, feminine products and diapers

Wipes labeled flushable do not break down like paper. They knot together in the trap and, further down, in the sewer lateral where roots and rough pipe walls snag them. Over months, a steady habit of flushing wipes can build a mat in a lateral.

What the city and county say to leave out of the drain

The City of Shelbyville runs its own Water Resource Recovery Facility and publishes a Sewer Line Blockage page on its website. That page is the place to check what the city asks residents to keep out of the sewer, and who to call if a blockage is in the public main.

If your home is on a septic system rather than city sewer, the Shelby County Health Department Environmental Division handles septic permits and inspects repairs. Septic tanks suffer more from wipes, grease and harsh cleaners, since solids pile up in the tank and a clog can reach the leach field.

Source type: City of Shelbyville website (sewer and blockage pages) and Shelby County Health Department Environmental Division.

How to plunge properly

1

Choose a flange plunger

The flat cup type is for sinks. A toilet needs the cup with the extra rubber flap that fits the drain opening.

2

Set the water level

There should be enough water to cover the cup. If the bowl is low, add water from a bucket. If it is near the rim, bail some out first.

3

Seal and push gently first

Push the first stroke slowly to expel air, then pump 10 to 15 times with a firm seal. Quick, hard strokes splash and break the seal.

4

Watch for the drop

When the water swirls away, pour a bucket in to test. A strong drop means it is clear. A slow drain means the blockage is still there.

5

Stop after a few rounds

If three rounds fail, a closet auger (a short toilet snake with a protected tip) is the next tool. Beyond that, call before you risk scratching the porcelain.

When the problem is the toilet, not the flush

Check the tank. If the water level sits an inch below the overflow tube, the bowl gets less water than designed. Raise the float or adjust the fill valve until the line meets the mark inside the tank.

Rim jets under the lip can be clogged with mineral scale. A mirror and a small wire show whether the holes are open. Scale is a reason to test your tap for hardness, and our toilet repair visits include checking these parts, along with the flapper and fill valve, which are short parts jobs rather than a new toilet.

Toilet performance is tested to ASME A112.19.2, the standard for ceramic plumbing fixtures. EPA WaterSense labels toilets that use 1.28 gallons or less per flush. A WaterSense model that clogs often is a bad fit or a bad drain, so check the line before blaming the label.

Before you call about a repeat clog

These checks help the plumber decide whether it is a toilet fix or a drain line job.

  • Note how many days apart the clogs are
  • Check whether other drains gurgle when you flush
  • Look at the base for rocking, leaking or a wax smell
  • Count how many people use the toilet and what goes in
  • Do not use chemical drain cleaner, which can burn a technician's skin

When repeat clogs point to the sewer line

If every toilet in the house is slow, or the lowest drain in a basement bubbles when you run the washer, the problem sits in the main line. Roots, a bellied pipe or a heavy grease coating in a lateral can all do this. Cabling clears the stoppage, and a camera shows why it keeps returning.

Our sewer line cleaning work uses a cable machine first, then hydro jetting where scale or grease is the issue. After that, a camera pass shows whether the pipe has a break or a low spot that will clog again.
