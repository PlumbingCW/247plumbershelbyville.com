Frozen and Burst Pipes in Shelbyville, IN: Winter Playbook

If a pipe in your Shelbyville, IN home is frozen or burst, find the main shutoff first and close it. Then thaw slowly with gentle heat, never a flame.

A split pipe is a job for burst pipe repair. A frozen but intact pipe may only need careful thawing.

Do these four things in order

Whether the pipe is frozen or already split, start here.

1

Close the main water shutoff

Turn the valve fully off, usually near where the line enters the house or by the water meter. If water is already spraying, this stops the damage.

2

Open the faucets

Open a faucet on the same line, cold and hot, to relieve pressure and let melting water drain once it thaws.

3

Stay clear of wet electrics

If water reaches outlets, panels or appliances, leave and call a licensed electrician. Do not stand in water to reach a panel.

4

Call the 24/7 line

Tell dispatch whether the pipe is frozen or leaking. A licensed plumber can repair the break and check the rest of the line.

Frozen is not the same as burst

A frozen pipe is blocked by ice but may be whole. A burst pipe has split, often at the point where the ice expanded, and leaks once the ice melts. That is why a pipe that seems fine while frozen can flood the house an hour after it thaws.

Shutting off the main before you thaw anything is the safe default in a Shelbyville winter. Guidelines from the American Red Cross and the Insurance Information Institute both advise shutting off the main water valve first and opening taps to relieve pressure.

Where pipes freeze in Indiana homes

Exterior walls

Kitchen and bath supply lines inside an outside wall have little insulation between them and the cold.

Crawlspaces and rim joists

Cold air leaks around the sill plate, so lines running along it chill first.

Garages and unheated utility rooms

A water heater or laundry line in a garage has no heat unless you provide it.

Hose bibs and outdoor lines

A hose left attached traps water in the bib. Frozen water splits the valve from inside the wall.

Attic and ceiling runs

Lines above insulation sit in the coldest air when insulation is pushed over them.

Thaw it safely

Safe ways to thaw

  • Hair dryer on low, moving along the pipe
  • Warm towels wrapped around the pipe
  • Small space heater, never left unattended
  • Open the faucet so melting water can drain

Never do this

  • Open flame such as a torch or lighter
  • Propane or kerosene heaters in a closed space
  • Boiling water poured on plastic or brittle pipe
  • Leaving a heater near insulation or wet floors

Start at the faucet end and work back toward the frozen spot, so melting water has a path out. If the pipe is in a wall or under a slab, stop and call a plumber instead of cutting open drywall yourself. Heat on a plastic pipe or near insulation can start a fire.

The 30 inch frost line

The City of Shelbyville's building codes page lists a frost line depth of 30 inches under its climatic and geographic design criteria. That is how deep the ground can freeze, and buried pipes are meant to sit below it.

A service line laid shallower than 30 inches, or one with little soil cover where it enters a foundation, is a candidate for freezing. If you find a recurring frozen line, ask a plumber about the burial depth. Call 811 before anyone digs, at least two business days ahead.

Prevent the next freeze

Do these before the hardest cold arrives.

  • Disconnect and drain garden hoses, then shut the bib's indoor valve
  • Seal gaps at the rim joist and around pipe entries
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls during a hard freeze
  • Let a faucet drip when the forecast is severe
  • Know where your main shutoff is and test the handle
  • Keep the thermostat steady if you travel
