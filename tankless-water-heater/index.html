Who tankless fits, and who it does not

A tankless heater fires a burner or energizes heating elements only when a hot tap opens. There is no 40 gallon reservoir keeping water warm around the clock, so the unit can last longer and take less floor space. Many Shelbyville, Indiana homeowners ask about it after a tank fails.

It works best when you can feed it properly. Gas units often need a larger gas line and a stainless or PVC vent. Electric whole-home units can need a heavy electrical service that some panels cannot supply.

Tankless usually suits

  • Households that run out of hot water with a standard tank
  • Homes with space that is tight for a tank
  • Owners ready to pay more up front for a longer-lived unit
  • Houses where a gas line and vent route can be run

Tankless is a harder sell when

  • The existing gas line is undersized and runs are long
  • The electrical panel cannot carry an electric unit
  • The vent route is blocked or code prohibits it
  • You only need hot water at one remote tap

The planning that decides whether it works

Flow rate and temperature rise

Gallons per minute you need at the winter inlet temperature set the model. Indiana groundwater is cold in January, so the heater must raise it a long way.

Gas line capacity

A whole-home unit can draw several times the BTUs of a tank. The line from the meter must carry it, and half-inch pipe often cannot.

Venting material and route

Condensing models use PVC or polypropylene vent through a sidewall. Non-condensing models need stainless steel.

Condensate drain

Condensing units make acidic condensate that goes to a drain, often through a neutralizer.

Isolation valves

Service valves on both water lines make flushing possible. Without them, descaling is a bigger job.

If you are comparing against a standard tank, our water heater installation page covers that route, and a tank that has already failed is handled under replacement.

DIY limits on tankless installs

A tankless install involves gas piping, vent assembly and sometimes new electrical circuits. Gas leaks and carbon monoxide leaks are not forgiving mistakes. In the city of Shelbyville, only registered plumbing contractors can apply for plumbing permits, and homeowners working on a home they own and live in must get a permit first. Elsewhere in Shelby County, ask the building department.

Gas pipe size comes from sizing tables such as those in NFPA 54, the National Fuel Gas Code, and gas tankless units are certified to ANSI Z21.10.3. The practical point is that the maker's manual and those tables set the pipe and vent, not a guess. Test your carbon monoxide alarm on every level after the install.

Tankless repair: error codes and cold sandwiches

Most tankless calls start with a blinking code. Typical causes are a scaled heat exchanger, a clogged inlet filter, a blocked vent or condensate drain, a failed igniter, a flame rod that needs cleaning or a flow sensor stuck from debris.

The cold sandwich is another classic. Hot water stops for a few seconds between two bursts of hot because the unit shut off and fired again. Short draws that never reach the minimum flow rate cause it, and so can low flow from a clogged aerator.

Flush and descale: the yearly job that saves the heat exchanger

Tankless heaters run hotter in a narrower channel than a tank, so mineral scale shows up faster. Test your tap, and ask Indiana American Water for its annual water quality report to see what your supply carries. In many homes a yearly flush is the sensible interval, and a water softener changes that.

The process uses a small pump, two hoses and about 3 gallons of white vinegar or a descaling solution circulated through the isolation ports for 45 minutes or more. Then we rinse the system and clean the inlet filter.

Homes in Shelbyville with heavy scale may want treatment at the main. See water softener and filtration for how that works.

Safety checklist for a gas or electric tankless unit

Know these before the unit is running.

  • If you smell gas, leave the house, do not touch switches, and call the gas utility's emergency line from outside.
  • A carbon monoxide alarm should be on each level, and it should be tested after any gas appliance is installed.
  • Keep the vent and the area around the unit clear, and do not store paint thinner or aerosols nearby.
  • Turn off the breaker before anyone opens an electric unit, and leave wiring changes to a licensed electrician.
  • If water is leaking from the unit, shut off its water supply valve and the power or gas, then call.
