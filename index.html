When a plumbing problem cannot wait

An emergency plumber call in Shelbyville, Indiana is any plumbing problem that is damaging the house or making it unsafe right now. Water you cannot stop, sewage rising in a floor drain, no heat during a freeze and a tank leaking onto the floor all qualify. A slow bathroom sink does not, and it can wait for a daytime visit.

Old downtown pipes and West Side houses

The Shelbyville Commercial Historic District holds 149 contributing buildings, developed from about 1822 to the 1930s, roughly between Broadway, Tompkins, Mechanic and Noble Streets. Buildings that old often have cast iron, galvanized steel and newer PVC drain pieces joined end to end, so a stoppage can sit at a joint between materials. A sewer camera inspection shows which pipe is failing before anyone cuts into a floor.

The West Side Historic District grew from about 1853 to 1939 and counts 373 contributing buildings in Queen Anne and Colonial Revival styles. Houses from that era can still have galvanized supply lines that rust shut from the inside, which shows up as weak flow and brown water. If yours is one of them, ask about repiping before you patch one leak at a time.

Two rivers meet here, and basements feel it

Shelbyville sits at the fork of the Big Blue and Little Blue Rivers. The Big Blue crested at 19.98 feet on April 6, 2025, the second-highest crest on record and the highest since 1913, according to the National Weather Service. Low ground near the water means a high water table, and a basement there needs a sump pit that works before the next high water, not after.

The city's flood page says yards near the Big Blue River begin to flood at 16.3 feet and homes near CR 100N and Boggstown Road at 17 feet. It also links the FEMA flood zone map, so look up your address. If you live in a spot like that, a tested sump pump with a backup and a look at basement waterproofing are worth sorting out now.

Winter adds its own risk. The city lists a frost line of 30 inches, so buried water lines need cover below that depth, and pipes in garages, crawlspaces and exterior walls freeze first. When one splits, closing the main and calling the 24/7 line beats chasing the water with towels. Any dig for a buried line needs a call to Indiana 811 at least two business days ahead. The burst pipe repair page covers the options.

Checking any plumber before you let them in

Indiana licenses plumbing contractors through the Indiana Plumbing Commission. The state's license lookup at mylicense.in.gov can be searched by name, license number, city or ZIP, so run any plumber through it, including us. Do it from your phone while they are in the driveway. The city lists the 2012 Indiana Plumbing Code on its building codes page, and a permitted job is measured against that code.

Permits matter too. In the city of Shelbyville, only registered plumbing contractors can apply for plumbing permits, and a homeowner working on their own home must get a permit before starting. A plumber who offers to skip a permit on work that needs one deserves a second question. Rules in outlying towns vary, so ask the local building department.
