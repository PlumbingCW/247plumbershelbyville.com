Your tank gave out: what happens next

Water heater replacement is removing a failed tank, setting a new one of the right size and fuel type, and reconnecting the water, gas or electric, venting and safety valves to code. In Shelbyville, Indiana the tank is usually in a basement, garage or utility closet, which decides how fast the job goes.

A typical failure shows up as a puddle that grows or a rusty stream at the base of the jacket. You will hear a plumber ask three things first: gas or electric, what size is the label, and where does the water go if it spills.

Do this before the plumber gets there

Short steps that protect your floor and your family.

1

Shut off the fuel

Turn the breaker off or the gas control to OFF. If you smell gas, get everyone outside, call 911 and your gas utility, and then call us.

2

Stop the cold water

Close the valve on the cold line above the tank. If that valve will not turn or leaks, find the main shutoff where the line enters the house.

3

Save the label photo

Take a picture of the data plate on the side of the tank. Capacity, input rating and model number let dispatch line up a matching unit before the truck rolls.

4

Clear a path

Move boxes, shelves and laundry baskets away from the heater and the stairs. A 50 gallon tank weighs about 400 pounds when full, and we need room to walk the old one out.

Whatever you choose, set the new thermostat at no more than 120 degrees Fahrenheit, the level the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission advises to reduce scald risk.

What a same-day swap looks like

1

Confirm size and fuel

Most homes get a like-for-like tank, usually 40 or 50 gallons. A larger household or a soaker tub may justify stepping up.

2

Drain and disconnect

The old tank is drained to a floor drain or outside by hose. Water lines, the gas line or electrical whip, and the flue are disconnected.

3

Set and plumb the new heater

New flexible connectors or copper, a fresh T&P relief valve with a discharge line, and a drain pan where the space calls for one.

4

Vent, fire up and test

On gas, the flue gets a draft check and every joint a leak test. On electric, the tank is filled completely before power goes on, so the elements never run dry.

5

Haul the old tank away

We carry out the failed unit so you are not left with a rusted tank in the garage.

Why the tank must be full before electric power goes on

An electric element burns out in seconds if it heats air instead of water. We open a hot tap and let it run steadily before flipping the breaker, which purges air from the tank and protects the new elements.

New tanks carry their test standard on the label. Typical home gas storage heaters are certified to ANSI Z21.10.1 and electric tanks are listed to UL 174. The temperature and pressure relief valve is made to ANSI Z21.22 and needs a discharge line piped toward the floor, never capped. Gas piping and venting follow NFPA 54, the National Fuel Gas Code, so every joint is leak tested and the draft is checked before we leave.

Choosing the replacement

Standard tank

  • Lowest first cost of the three common types
  • Same hookup as most existing heaters
  • Gas or electric
  • Holds a fixed supply and recovers slowly after a big load

Other routes

  • Tankless heats on demand and needs gas line and venting review
  • Hybrid heat pump models use less electricity but need airflow and a condensate drain
  • Both usually cost more up front and take more planning than a straight swap

In an emergency most people want the same type back quickly. If you would like to upgrade, a planned job is cheaper to engineer, and our water heater installation page covers venting, expansion tanks and permits in detail.

Flooded basements and slab-level heaters in Shelby County

Shelbyville sits at the fork of the Big Blue and Little Blue Rivers, and the city flood page warns that low ground near the Big Blue can take water in high river stages. A heater sitting at slab level in a flood-prone basement can be ruined by water long before the tank wears out. If yours was submerged, even a clean-looking unit should be replaced, because gas valves and thermostats do not recover from soaking.

Raising a replacement tank on a pedestal a foot or so above the floor is a simple improvement during a swap.

What drives the price of a replacement

Two homes can both get a 50 gallon heater and see very different totals. These are the reasons.

Tank type and sizeA basic 40 gallon electric is the baseline. Larger tanks, high-efficiency gas units and hybrids step up.
Venting changesReusing a sound chimney flue is simple. A new liner, power vent or relocated pipe adds parts and labor.
Code upgradesAn expansion tank, a drain pan, a properly piped T&P discharge line and working shutoff valves are common additions on older hookups.
Permit and inspectionSome jurisdictions need a permit for a heater swap. We handle the paperwork where it applies.
Haul-away and timingDisposal of the old tank and same-day or after-hours scheduling can change the total. Ask when you call.

Insurance usually pays for sudden water damage to floors and belongings from a burst tank but not for the heater itself, which is treated as normal wear. Photograph everything before cleanup and read your policy for water damage wording.

Permits and who may pull them

In the city of Shelbyville, only registered plumbing contractors can apply for plumbing permits, and a homeowner working on a home they own and live in must get a permit first. The work is held to the Indiana Plumbing Code, which the city lists as the 2012 edition. Elsewhere in Shelby County the rule can differ, so ask the building department. A licensed plumber handles the permit so your new heater is on record.
